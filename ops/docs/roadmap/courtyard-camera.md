# Courtyard and camera composition

Ouk Chaktrang now offers two coordinated collections: Atelier (boxwood and rosewood) and Courtyard (sandstone and charcoal). The compact picker previews a native Khon and opposing Ses, selects both 2D and 3D together, and stores the choice only for Cambodia. The first-use default remains Atelier. Material buttons name the current collection's original finish correctly. Draughts keeps its three choices and independent preferences.

Compact Artwork, Clear and Letters controls retain Cambodia's simpler 2D representations. A separate guarded preference migrates existing Clear Khmer/Khmer letters selections and keeps them independent of board colour and 3D collection. Artwork uses the selected collection's generated sprites.

Courtyard has twelve individually generated transparent masters and 512px lossless WebP sprites. Its separate 5,883,804-byte GLB contains twelve semantic roots, four PBR materials and seven embedded images. UVs, texture maps and geometry are portable; no external texture URL is required. The largest piece is 74mm tall. The Ses muzzle/mane spans 42.5mm over a 34.18mm foot, within the 50mm board square. Other complete pieces stay below 40mm wide. The most complex piece has 8,944 triangles.

The octagonal feet, recessed shaft panels, overlapping lotus petals, open bowl and recessed counter top are geometry. Editable Blender source, original stone shader nodes, packed maps, build script, generated masters and exact prompts are retained. This is a contemporary native-inspired collection, not a historical replica. Ses adapts Tina's CC0 Horse Head and retains its swept mane; the 2D master uses a shorter ridged mane. Provenance and reproduction instructions: `ops/assets/khmer/courtyard/README.md`.

Camera composition now uses actual delivered piece heights and physical case/tray bounds. The initial stage aspect follows that composition and remains stable while orbiting. At a 320px viewport, International draughts' 296px-wide canvas falls from 308px to 210px high (98px less empty vertical space) without reducing horizontal board fill. These are canvas dimensions, not the entire controls wrapper. A bounding-sphere distance envelope allows Shogi's trays to remain visible when rotated across the vertical axis.

Shogi's tray envelope includes a selected stack of three captured tiles, with a 44mm allowance. Independent review caught an initial empty-tray-only bound; the corrected geometry and orbit regressions cover both hand trays in full and mini Shogi.

Edge coordinates are small, camera-facing text anchored to their board-edge screen position. Cap height is 8 CSS pixels below a 520px canvas width, 10 pixels above it. They retain depth testing and use a fine dark glyph stroke on non-Japanese boards for contrast; there are no filled badges. Free zoom/pan and reset behavior remain intact.
